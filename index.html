Walton County, Florida

Coastal Dune Lakes of Walton County

Coastal dune lakes are rare, and they are considered imperiled. Along roughly 26 miles of this Gulf coast, Walton County holds about 15 named examples, a short and unusual chain.

Each lake sits in the low ground behind the beach. Most of the time sand keeps the Gulf out. Now and then an outfall opens, the water turns brackish, and then the cut closes and the lake begins to freshen. This site explains that pattern and gives a page to every lake from Fuller Lake in the west to Lake Powell on the line with Bay County.

How these lakes work

Rare, and gathered on one coast

A basin of fresh to brackish water, held in coastal dunes and opening to the sea only now and then, is an uncommon kind of lake, and an imperiled one. Walton County’s stretch of the Florida Panhandle is unusual because the lakes are not scattered one-offs. About 15 named lakes lie along roughly 26 miles of shoreline.

They share a setting, not a single size or a single owner. Campbell Lake is the clearest intact case in this set: watershed, lake, and outfall all sit in protected state land at Topsail. Stallworth Lake is about 15 acres, with a largely private shore. Lake Powell, on the line with Bay County, is the giant of the chain. Choctawhatchee Basin Alliance morphometry puts it near 710 acres. Some park descriptions say nearly 800. The entire lake is an Outstanding Florida Water.

Fresh, then brackish

Rain and shallow groundwater fill the basins. A berm of beach sand usually stands between the lake and the Gulf of Mexico. When the lake is high enough, or a storm cuts the beach, the berm can fail. Water runs out. Gulf water runs in. Salinity rises, and the lake becomes brackish. Marine life can use the channel. Sand later closes the cut, and freshwater inflows begin to freshen the lake again. The exchange is intermittent. These are not ordinary tidal bays, and they are not ponds that never taste salt.

Fuller Lake does not fit the outfall pattern. It has no direct opening of its own. It is hydrologically connected with Morris Lake, the next lake east, in the dunes at Topsail. Allen Lake shows a different human arrangement: it sits in view of County Road 30A, and its drainage to the Gulf passes under the road. Western Lake, about 244 acres and several lobes wide, is where most people can watch an outfall open and close.

Watched in partnership

Walton County partners with the Choctawhatchee Basin Alliance to monitor the lakes. Level, salinity, and whether an outfall is open all move with rain, groundwater, and storms. One afternoon at the shore is a poor summary of a lake’s year.

The acreages in this guide are approximate for the same reason published numbers disagree. Studies trace the shoreline differently, and a lake does not hold one permanent outline. Where a second figure is widely repeated, the lake page includes it. Nothing here is a survey, a water-quality report, or an official park brochure.
